# MP2-V: attenuated MP2 with a VV10 tail {#mp2-v}

<!-- status: edit; registry: mp2-v -->

Attenuated MP2 (section @sec:attenuated-mp2) cuts the electron–electron interaction off
beyond a length $r_0$, and with it the dispersion attraction between
distant fragments: its long-range $C_6$ coefficients are zero. MP2-V adds
that tail back with the VV10 nonlocal correlation functional, damped at
short range so it does not count again what the attenuated MP2 already
holds (Goldey, Belzunces and Head-Gordon, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 11, 4159
(2015)); the dissertation (§7.2.2) names this pairing as the next step for
attenuated MP2 and the compatible short-range damping as its main
difficulty. With the tail present the attenuation can be harder: MP2-V
uses $r_0 = 1.00$ Å at aug-cc-pVTZ, where plain attenuated MP2 uses
1.35 Å.

The plan: the energy and its damping (@sec:mp2-v-energy), the fitted parameters
(@sec:mp2-v-parameters), where they apply (@sec:mp2-v-scope-parameterization), and what is anchored (@sec:mp2-v-anchored).

## The energy {#mp2-v-energy}

The energy has three terms: the Hartree–Fock energy, a short-range MP2
correlation energy, and a long-range dispersion energy from the
Hartree–Fock density:

$$ E_{\text{MP2-V}} = E_{\mathrm{HF}} + E_c^{\mathrm{att}}\big[\operatorname{terfc}(r; r_0)/r\big] + E_{\mathrm{nl}}\big[\rho_{\mathrm{HF}};\, b, C, r_0\big] . \label{eq:mp2-v-1} $$

The first correlation term is section @sec:attenuated-mp2's terfc RI-MP2 (UMP2 for an
open-shell reference), with the operator of (@eq:mp2-att-terfc-kernel) at the linked width
$\omega = 1/(\sqrt2\,r_0)$ (section @sec:erf-terf-curvature-constraint). The closed-shell path sums
$E_{\mathrm{OS}} + E_{\mathrm{SS}}$, the open-shell path
$E_{\alpha\alpha} + E_{\beta\beta} + E_{\alpha\beta}$; both are the
spin-orbital MP2 energy of the attenuated integrals. The operator comes
from `ferric-mp2/src/att_vv10.rs::mp2_operator`: `Operator::terfc` at the
linked $\omega$ when `omega` is `None`, `Operator::terfc_with_omega`
otherwise, and for the erfc control `Operator::erfc` at the same linked
$\omega$.

The second term is VV10 on the Hartree–Fock total density
$\rho = \rho_\alpha + \rho_\beta$, written as a grid sum

$$ E_{\mathrm{nl}} = \sum_g w_g\,\rho_g\Big(\beta + \tfrac12 f_g\Big), \qquad f_g = \sum_{g'} w_{g'}\,\rho_{g'}\,\Phi(g, g')\,d(R_{gg'}), \qquad \beta = \frac{1}{32}\Big(\frac{3}{b^2}\Big)^{3/4} , \label{eq:mp2-v-2} $$

with $\Phi$ the VV10 kernel of Vydrov and Van Voorhis (section @sec:vv10) and $R_{gg'}$ the distance between two grid
points. $\beta$ is a local constant: it multiplies each point's own
density and involves no second point. $f_g$ is the nonlocal pair sum. It
is negative, and it is the only place two points interact. VV10 is a
functional of the total density alone, so the open-shell path calls the
same evaluator on $\rho_\alpha + \rho_\beta$ and needs no spin-resolved
VV10. The density is `ferric-scf/src/result.rs::density_total`, which is
$D_\alpha + D_\beta$ for a UHF or ROHF reference and $2D_\alpha$ for RHF,
the spin-free electron density in both paths. Both entry points end in one
shared function, `ferric-mp2/src/att_vv10.rs::assemble`, which evaluates
VV10 on that density and forms the sum (@eq:mp2-v-1), so the closed- and
open-shell paths make the same VV10 call.

### Why the damping is $1 - \operatorname{terfc}^2$

How much dispersion does attenuated MP2 keep at a separation $R$? Take two
fragments far apart. Expand the attenuated operator between them in
multipoles: every term carries the factor $\operatorname{terfc}(R; r_0)$,
because the attenuation varies slowly over the size of each fragment when
$R$ is large (the paper's Eq. 9). The MP2 dispersion energy is second order
in that operator, one factor to excite the pair and one to return it, so
it carries the factor twice (the paper's Eq. 10):

$$ E^{\text{att. MP2}}_{\text{disp}} = -\operatorname{terfc}(R; r_0)^2\,\frac{C_6^{\mathrm{MP2}}}{R^6} . $$

Attenuated MP2 therefore holds the fraction $\operatorname{terfc}^2$ of the
dispersion at distance $R$. VV10 has to supply the rest, the fraction
$1 - \operatorname{terfc}^2$. That is the damping on the pair kernel:

$$ d(R) = 1 - \operatorname{terfc}(R; r_0)^2 . \label{eq:mp2v-damping} $$

The same $r_0$ appears in (@eq:mp2-v-1) and (@eq:mp2v-damping); it is one parameter, not two.
In the paper's words, the $r_0$ parameter "is shared with the attenuated
short-range MP2 part rather than being adjusted separately".

Check the limits. At $R = 0$ the two error functions in terfc are
$\operatorname{erfc}(-\omega r_0)$ and $\operatorname{erfc}(\omega r_0)$,
which sum to 2, so $\operatorname{terfc}(0) = 1$ and $d(0) = 0$: VV10 adds
nothing where attenuated MP2 keeps the full interaction. At large $R$,
$\operatorname{terfc} \to 0$ and $d \to 1$: plain VV10. In between, at the
linked width, $d(r_0) = 0.727$, $d(2r_0) = 0.974$ and $d(3r_0) = 0.9995$
(computed from (@eq:mp2-att-terfc-kernel)). So VV10 contributes where the attenuated MP2
kernel has died away (the dashed curve in section @sec:attenuated-mp2's figure). Only the
pair kernel is damped. $\beta$ carries no pair distance and is not.

What happens to the sign? Damping scales the negative $f_g$ toward zero, so
$f_g$ becomes less negative and the absolute $E_{\mathrm{nl}}$ rises. The
dispersion binding is a difference between complex and monomers, not the
sign of the absolute number.

Water in cc-pVDZ, from the `18375bead` build, with and without the damping
(`FERRIC_TERF_TABLE_DIR` points at the repository's `terf-tables/`, because the
correlation term is terfc-attenuated). Damping raises $E_{\mathrm{nl}}$ by
0.47 mEh:

```python
import os

os.environ.setdefault("FERRIC_TERF_TABLE_DIR", "terf-tables")  # the terfc interpolation tables
import ferric

mol = ferric.Molecule.from_xyz("testdata/molecules/water.xyz")
bs = ferric.BasisSet.bundled("cc-pvdz")
aux = ferric.BasisSet.bundled("cc-pvdz-ri")

damped = ferric.run_mp2_v(mol, bs, aux, frozen_core=1)                       # r0 = 1.00 A, b = 11.0, C = 0.0089, terfc
bare = ferric.run_mp2_v(mol, bs, aux, frozen_core=1, vv10_damping="none")    # same, VV10 undamped
print(f"E_HF          = {damped.rhf_energy:.8f} Ha")
print(f"E_c^att       = {damped.att_mp2_corr:.8f} Ha  (E_OS {damped.e_os:.8f}, E_SS {damped.e_ss:.8f})")
print(f"E_nl damped   = {damped.vv10_e_nl:.8f} Ha")
print(f"E_nl undamped = {bare.vv10_e_nl:.8f} Ha  (damped minus undamped {damped.vv10_e_nl - bare.vv10_e_nl:+.8f})")
print(f"E_MP2-V       = {damped.total_energy:.8f} Ha  on {damped.n_nlc_points} NLC grid points")
```

```text
E_HF          = -76.02676800 Ha
E_c^att       = -0.20066861 Ha  (E_OS -0.15005668, E_SS -0.05061193)
E_nl damped   = 0.01863863 Ha
E_nl undamped = 0.01816780 Ha  (damped minus undamped +0.00047082)
E_MP2-V       = -76.20879798 Ha  on 7500 NLC grid points
```

A second consequence of $d(0) = 0$: the pair sum contains the self-pair
$g = g'$ at $R = 0$, which is fully damped for every $r_0 > 0$. As
$r_0 \to 0$ the damped energy therefore approaches a small floor below
undamped VV10, not undamped VV10 itself (`ferric-dft/src/vv10.rs::Vv10Damping`
records 9.05e-6 Ha, 0.05% of $E_{\mathrm{nl}}$, on water/cc-pVDZ for every
$r_0 \le 10^{-3}$ Bohr). For undamped VV10 use the damping switch `"none"`,
not a tiny $r_0$.

### The damping in the code

The factor is evaluated per pair of grid points from the squared distance.
This is `Vv10Damping::factor_from_r2` with its comments replaced:

```rust
match *self {
    Vv10Damping::None => 1.0,                          // bare VV10: d = 1
    Vv10Damping::Terfc {
        r0_bohr,
        omega_bohr_inv,
    } => {
        let r_one = damping_r_one(r0_bohr, omega_bohr_inv);   // r0 + 5/ω
        if r2 > r_one * r_one {
            return 1.0;                                // terfc < 1e-12: d rounds to 1.0
        }
        let tc = terfc_dispatch(r2.sqrt(), r0_bohr, omega_bohr_inv);   // terfc(R; r0)
        1.0 - tc * tc                                  // d(R), equation 10.21
    }
}
```

- **The argument is $R^2$.** The pair loop already has it, and the `None`
  arm does not pay for a square root.
- **The cutoff is exact.** With the linked width, $1/\omega = \sqrt2\,r_0$
  and $r_{\mathrm{one}} = (1 + 5\sqrt2)\,r_0 \approx 8.07\,r_0$, where terfc
  is $7.7\times10^{-13}$. Past it the skip returns the same bits the full
  formula would.
- **The factor multiplies the whole pair contribution.** It depends on the
  pair distance only, not on $\rho$ or $\nabla\rho$, so the same factor
  carries through the chain rule to the potentials.
- **One $\omega$ for both halves.** `AttVv10Config::effective_vv10_damping`
  copies the MP2 operator's $\omega$ into the damping and returns an error
  if the damping already carries a different one.

## Parameters {#mp2-v-parameters}

The published method is MP2-V(terfc, aTZ): fitted on S66 at aug-cc-pVTZ,
frozen core, no counterpoise, with the SG-1 grid.

| Parameter | Published | ferric default | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| $r_0$ | 1.00 Å (1.890 Bohr) | 1.00 Å | `mp2v_r0`, Å |
| $b$ | 11.0 | 11.0 | moves with $r_0$ (table below) |
| $C$ | 0.0089 | 0.0089 | fixed at the LC-VV10 value, not fitted |
| attenuator | terfc | terfc | `"erfc"` is a control, not the method |
| frozen core | yes | CLI and Python: 0 (Rust: 0) | set it; a notice is printed at 0 |
| grid | SG-1 | 50 radial × 50 angular NLC grid | ferric has no SG-1 |
| VV10 in the Fock matrix | optional in the paper | no: post-HF only | the paper reports both as equivalent |

$b$ sets how strongly VV10's own kernel is damped at short range; it enters
$\Phi$ and $\beta$ of (@eq:mp2-v-2). $C$ sets the size of the $C_6$
coefficients. The paper fixes $C$ at the LC-VV10 value, which it says
ensures high-quality long-range $C_6$ values, and scans $r_0$ and $b$ on a
grid.

$r_0$ and $b$ are correlated. The paper's fit valley, available as
`AttVv10Config::mp2_v_terfc_atz_at_r0`:

| $r_0$ (Å) | 0.85 | 0.90 | 0.95 | 1.00 | 1.05 | 1.10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $b$ | 8.0 | 9.0 | 9.5 | 11.0 | 12.5 | 14.5 |

Changing `mp2v_r0` without moving `mp2v_b` leaves the fitted valley.

A free seam width `mp2v_omega` (Å⁻¹) replaces the curvature link in both
the MP2 kernel and the damping together; an $\omega$ set on one and not the
other is an error. Any free-$\omega$ run is unparameterized: $b$ was fitted
at the linked width.

The erfc control uses $\operatorname{erfc}(\omega r)/r$ with the same
linked $\omega = 1/(\sqrt2\,r_0)$, so the two attenuators are compared at
the same $r_0$. A free $\omega$ is refused for the erfc control.

The CLI prints a notice for each departure from the fitted protocol: frozen
core 0 or nonstandard, a basis other than aug-cc-pVTZ, the erfc attenuator,
an open-shell reference.

## Scope of the parameterization {#mp2-v-scope-parameterization}

| Use | Status |
|---|---|
| Closed-shell noncovalent interactions, aug-cc-pVTZ, no counterpoise | the fitted method |
| Other bases | unparameterized; no MP2-V(aDZ) was published |
| Counterpoise-corrected interaction energies | a convention mismatch |
| Open-shell references | unparameterized: S66 is all closed shell |
| Bonded interactions (reaction energies) | not recommended by the paper |

For orientation, the paper reports S66 RMSDs (kcal/mol, aug-cc-pVTZ, no
counterpoise) of 1.533 for MP2, 0.251 for MP2(terfc, aTZ) and 0.199 for
MP2-V(terfc, aTZ), with 0.202 for the post-HF variant ferric runs. These
are the paper's figures, not ferric anchors.

## Known limits (2026-11-15)

- Energy only; no gradient and no VV10 term in the Fock matrix.
- Python `run_mp2_v` is closed-shell; open shell is CLI or Rust.
- No SG-1 grid.
