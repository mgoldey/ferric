# VV10 nonlocal correlation {#vv10}

<!-- status: ready; registry: ks-vv10 -->

Two neutral molecules far apart still attract: the fluctuations of their
electron densities correlate across the gap, and the London dispersion
energy that results falls off as $R^{-6}$. A semilocal functional cannot
produce it, because between two separated molecules there is no density
for it to see. VV10 adds a correlation energy that is a double integral over
the density, with a kernel that couples every pair of points and decays as
$R^{-6}$. ferric evaluates it as a double sum over a separate coarse grid,
and gets the per-point potentials from one extra pass over the same pairs.

The plan: in @sec:vv10-energy-potentials, write the energy, check its two limits and derive the
per-point potentials; in @sec:vv10-algorithm, read the pair loop and bound the error of
its distance cutoff.

## Energy and potentials {#vv10-energy-potentials}

**The energy.** For a closed shell, on the total density, ferric evaluates
(`ferric-dft/src/vv10.rs::vv10_internal_screened`)

$$ E_{nl} = \sum_i W_i\rho_i\Bigl[\beta + \tfrac12 f_i\Bigr], \qquad f_i = \sum_p W_p\rho_p\,\Phi_{ip}, \qquad \Phi_{ip} = -\frac{3}{2\,g_i g_p (g_i+g_p)}, \qquad g_x = \omega_0(\mathbf r_x) R_{ip}^2 + \kappa(\mathbf r_x), $$

with $R_{ip}$ the distance between points $i$ and $p$ and

$$ \omega_0 = \sqrt{C\,(\sigma/\rho^2)^2 + \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho}, \qquad \kappa = b\,\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\Bigl(\frac{\rho}{9\pi}\Bigr)^{1/6}, \qquad \beta = \tfrac{1}{32}\bigl(3/b^2\bigr)^{3/4} . $$

The pair sum includes the self pair $p = i$ ($R = 0$).

Read the ingredients physically. $\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho = \omega_p^2/3$, where
$\omega_p^2 = 4\pi\rho$ is the plasma frequency of a uniform gas of that
density. $C(\sigma/\rho^2)^2 = C(\lvert\nabla\rho\rvert/\rho)^4$ is a
gradient term that raises $\omega_0$ where the density changes fast
relative to its own value, as it does in the tail of a molecule. $\kappa$ sets the kernel's value at short
range, and $\beta$ is a constant per electron.

Check two limits.

- **Large separation.** For $R_{ip} \to \infty$, $g_x \to \omega_0(\mathbf r_x)R_{ip}^2$
  and
  $$ \Phi_{ip} \to -\frac{3}{2\,\omega_{0,i}\,\omega_{0,p}\,(\omega_{0,i} + \omega_{0,p})\,R_{ip}^6} . $$
  The kernel decays as $R^{-6}$, the London form.
- **Uniform density.** With $\nabla\rho = 0$, $\omega_0 = \sqrt{4\pi\rho/3}$
  and $\kappa$ are the same everywhere, and in the continuum limit
  $f = -\tfrac34\rho\int 4\pi R^2(\omega_0R^2 + \kappa)^{-3}\,dR = -3\pi^2\rho/\bigl[16(\omega_0\kappa)^{3/2}\bigr]$.
  Substituting $\omega_0$ and $\kappa$ gives $\tfrac12 f = -\beta$ exactly, so
  $E_{nl} = 0$. $\beta$ is the constant that makes VV10 add nothing to the
  uniform electron gas.

**The potentials.** The SCF needs $\partial E_{nl}/\partial\rho_i$ and
$\partial E_{nl}/\partial\sigma_i$ at every point, to feed the GGA-form
matrix of section @sec:vxc. $\rho_i$ enters $E_{nl}$ in three places: the
prefactor $W_i\rho_i$, the partner weight $W_p\rho_p$ when $p = i$ in another
row, and $g_i$ through $\omega_0$ and $\kappa$. $\sigma_i$ enters only through
$\omega_0$.

1. **The prefactors.** The $\beta$ term gives $W_i\beta$. In
   $\tfrac12\sum_{i,p}W_i\rho_iW_p\rho_p\Phi_{ip}$, $\rho_i$ appears once as
   the row and once as a partner. $\Phi$ is symmetric, so the two terms are
   equal and cancel the $\tfrac12$: they give $W_if_i$.
2. **Through $g_i$.** Differentiate the kernel with respect to $g_i$,
   which appears in $g_i$ and in $g_t = g_i + g_p$:
   $$ \frac{\partial\Phi_{ip}}{\partial g_i} = \frac32\,\frac{1}{g_ig_pg_t}\Bigl(\frac1{g_i} + \frac1{g_t}\Bigr) . $$
   The same symmetry doubles this term too. The chain rule then needs
   $\partial g_i/\partial\rho_i = R_{ip}^2\,\partial\omega_0/\partial\rho + \partial\kappa/\partial\rho$.

Collect the pair sums. With $t_{ip} = W_p\rho_p/(g_ig_pg_t)$,

$$ u_i = \sum_p t_{ip}\Bigl(\frac1{g_i} + \frac1{g_t}\Bigr), \qquad w_i = \sum_p t_{ip}\Bigl(\frac1{g_i} + \frac1{g_t}\Bigr)R_{ip}^2 , $$

the $R^2$-free part of $\partial g_i/\partial\rho_i$ multiplies $u_i$ and the
$R^2$ part multiplies $w_i$. The per-point potentials are

$$ v_{\rho,i} = \beta + f_i + \tfrac32\Bigl(u_i\,\rho\frac{\partial\kappa}{\partial\rho} + w_i\,\rho\frac{\partial\omega_0}{\partial\rho}\Bigr)_i, \qquad v_{\sigma,i} = \tfrac32\,w_i\,\Bigl(\rho\frac{\partial\omega_0}{\partial\sigma}\Bigr)_i, $$

so that $W_i v_{\rho,i} = \partial E_{nl}/\partial\rho_i$ and
$W_i v_{\sigma,i} = \partial E_{nl}/\partial\sigma_i$. The factor $\rho$ in
front of each partial derivative is the $\rho_i$ of the prefactor $W_i\rho_i$,
folded in.

The three partials are short. From $\omega_0^2 = C\sigma^2/\rho^4 + \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho$,
differentiate and multiply by $\rho$:
$\rho\,\partial\omega_0/\partial\rho = (\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho - 2C\sigma^2/\rho^4)/\omega_0$
and $\rho\,\partial\omega_0/\partial\sigma = C\sigma/(\rho^3\omega_0)$. From
$\kappa \propto \rho^{1/6}$, $\rho\,\partial\kappa/\partial\rho = \kappa/6$.

**The code, in three pieces.** First the per-point setup in
`vv10_internal_screened`. Points with $\rho < 10^{-8}$ return early and take
no part in the pair sum; for the rest:

<!-- excerpt: ferric-dft/src/vv10.rs::vv10_internal_screened -->
```rust
let w0tmp = c_vv * (s / (r * r)).powi(2);
let w0sq = w0tmp + pi43 * r;
p.w0 = w0sq.sqrt();
p.kp = k_vv * r.powf(1.0 / 6.0);
p.dk_dr = p.kp / 6.0;
p.dw0_dr = (0.5 * pi43 * r - 2.0 * w0tmp) / p.w0;
```

| Symbol | Code |
|---|---|
| $C(\sigma/\rho^2)^2$, $\omega_0^2$, $\omega_0$ | `w0tmp`, `w0sq`, `p.w0` |
| $\kappa$, $\rho\,\partial\kappa/\partial\rho$, $\rho\,\partial\omega_0/\partial\rho$ | `p.kp`, `p.dk_dr`, `p.dw0_dr` |

The next line sets `p.dw0_dg` $= \rho\,\partial\omega_0/\partial\sigma = C\sigma/(\rho^3\omega_0)$.
It is kept at every active point, including $\sigma \le 10^{-8}$: $\omega_0$
depends on $\sigma$ wherever $\rho$ is active, so dropping the term there
would leave part of $E_{nl}$'s $\sigma$-dependence out of $V_{nl}$, and
$V_{nl}$ would no longer be $\partial E_{nl}/\partial D$.

Second, one pair $(i, p)$ inside the pair loop. The $g$ values:

<!-- excerpt: ferric-dft/src/vv10.rs::vv10_internal_screened -->
```rust
let gp_val = r2 * w0[p] + kp[p];
let gi_val = r2 * w0i + ki;
let gt_val = gi_val + gp_val;
```

then, with `t` $= t_{ip}$, the three accumulators:

<!-- excerpt: ferric-dft/src/vv10.rs::vv10_internal_screened -->
```rust
*fi += t;
let t_u = t * (1.0 / gi_val + 1.0 / gt_val);
*ui += t_u;
*wi += t_u * r2;
```

`fi` accumulates $\sum_pt_{ip}$, which is positive. The row returns
`-1.5 * fi`, so $f_i = -\tfrac32\sum_pt_{ip}$: the $-\tfrac32$ of $\Phi$ is
applied once per row, not once per pair. $u_i$ and $w_i$ are returned
without it, which is why the potentials carry $+\tfrac32$.

Third, the per-point energy and potentials, after the pair sums:

<!-- excerpt: ferric-dft/src/vv10.rs::vv10_internal_screened -->
```rust
let exc_g = beta + 0.5 * f_g;
exc[g] = exc_g;
vrho[g] = beta + f_g + 1.5 * (u_g * dk_dr[g] + w_g * dw0_dr[g]);
vsig[g] = 1.5 * w_g * dw0_dg[g];
e_nl += grid[g].weight * rho[g] * exc_g;
```

| Symbol | Code |
|---|---|
| $\beta + \tfrac12f_i$ | `exc_g` |
| $\rho\,\partial\kappa/\partial\rho$, $\rho\,\partial\omega_0/\partial\rho$, $\rho\,\partial\omega_0/\partial\sigma$ | `dk_dr`, `dw0_dr`, `dw0_dg` |
| $E_{nl}$ | `e_nl` |

**Open shell and the Fock matrix.** A UKS run evaluates $E_{nl}$ on the total
density and adds the same $V_{nl}$ to both spin Fock matrices.
`ferric-dft/src/vv10.rs::add_vv10_scratch` assembles $V_{nl}$ in the
closed-shell GGA form of section @sec:vxc,
$wv_\rho\chi_\mu\chi_\nu + 2wv_\sigma\nabla\rho\cdot(\chi_\mu\nabla\chi_\nu + \chi_\nu\nabla\chi_\mu)$.

## The algorithm {#vv10-algorithm}

| Step | What | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | density, gradient on the VV10 grid (default flat $50\times50$, never pruned) | as section @sec:vxc |
| 2 | per active point ($\rho \ge 10^{-8}$): $\omega_0$, $\kappa$ and the three $\rho$-scaled derivatives | $O(N)$ |
| 3 | pair sums $f_i$, $u_i$, $w_i$ over partners within 40 Bohr, through a cell list of that edge (dense below `CELL_LIST_MIN_PTS`) | $O(N\cdot N_{\rm near})$ |
| 4 | $E_{nl}$, $v_\rho$, $v_\sigma$ per point | $O(N)$ |
| 5 | $V_{nl}$ in the closed-shell GGA form, added to the Fock matrix (both spins get the same $V_{nl}$ in UKS) | `add_vv10_scratch` |

**The distance cutoff.** The production pair sum drops pairs with
$R_{ip} > 40$ Bohr (`NLC_CUTOFF_BOHR`) on grids of at least
`CELL_LIST_MIN_PTS` active points. It bins the points into cells of that
edge and visits the 27 neighbouring cells of each point's cell. Below that
size it runs the dense sum, which is exact. Retained partners are visited in
ascending index order in both paths, so the two differ only by the omitted
pairs.

The omitted energy has a bound. Every active point has $g \ge \omega_0R^2$,
so

$$ \lvert\Phi_{ip}\rvert \le \frac{3}{2\,\omega_{0,i}\,\omega_{0,p}(\omega_{0,i} + \omega_{0,p})\,R_{ip}^6} . $$

Each $\omega_0 \ge \sqrt{a\rho}$ with $a = 4\pi/3$, so a low-density point,
which has a small $\omega_0$, also carries a small $\rho$ in the numerator.
Using $\omega_{0,i} + \omega_{0,p} \ge \sqrt{a\max(\rho_i, \rho_p)}$, the
density factors of one pair reduce to $\sqrt{\min(\rho_i, \rho_p)} \le (\rho_i\rho_p)^{1/4}$.
Summing over all pairs beyond the cutoff bounds the magnitude of the omitted
energy:

$$ \lvert\Delta E_{nl}\rvert \le \tfrac34\,a^{-3/2}R_{\rm cut}^{-6}\Bigl[\sum_g w_g\rho_g^{1/4}\Bigr]^2 . $$

The bracket $S = \sum_gw_g\rho_g^{1/4}$ grows linearly with the size of the
molecule; for water $S \approx 10$. At $R_{\rm cut} = 40$ Bohr the prefactor
$\tfrac34a^{-3/2}R_{\rm cut}^{-6}$ is $2.1\times10^{-11}$ Ha, so the bound is
$2.1\times10^{-9}$ Ha for water and stays below $10^{-8}$ Ha for
$S \lesssim 21$. The tests `cutoff_energy_bounded_water` and
`cutoff_energy_bounded_alkane20` measure the omitted energy directly and
assert it is below $10^{-8}$ Ha for water (dense path) and a 20-atom alkane
(cutoff active). The $R^{-6}$ decay makes the needed cutoff grow slowly:
doubling $S$ multiplies it by $2^{1/3} \approx 1.26$.

**Reproducibility and accuracy.** The 40 Bohr cutoff is a fixed constant,
not an option, so a configuration cannot loosen the bound. The nuclear
gradient of VV10 does not include the Becke-weight response of the VV10
grid, by design; it depends on `nlc_grid` and is not FD-validated on its own
(validation page, KS gradient on custom grids).
